New Jersey GDL Explained for Parents

A parent's guide to New Jersey's Graduated Driver License program: the special learner permit, 6 hours of professional training, 50 supervised hours, and the probationary license.

New Jersey's Graduated Driver License (GDL) program phases in driving privileges as teens gain experience. Here's how it works for a teen starting at 16, based on information published by the NJ MVC.

Step 1: Special learner permit (age 16)

  • Your teen must be at least 16, have signed parent or guardian consent, and be enrolled in an approved behind-the-wheel course through a licensed driving school or an approved school program.
  • They must pass the MVC knowledge test and vision screening.
  • The approved course is six full hours of behind-the-wheel instruction in a dual-controlled vehicle with a licensed instructor. Time spent at the MVC doesn't count toward those hours.
  • The MVC validates the permit for practice driving only after the course is completed. The permit is valid for two years.

Step 2: Supervised practice

For permits issued on or after February 1, 2025 to drivers under 21, New Jersey requires a minimum of 50 hours of supervised practice driving, including 10 hours in darkness, before a probationary license. The supervising driver must be at least 21 and hold a valid NJ license for the type of vehicle. A parent, guardian or supervising driver certifies the hours on MVC form BA-CSD. The MVC notes that submitting a fraudulent certification can lead to a six-month suspension.

Teens under 21 must wait at least six months from permit issuance before the road test.

Step 3: Probationary license (age 17+)

After at least six months of supervised driving, the 50 certified hours, and a passed road test, a teen who is at least 17 can get a probationary license. Probationary drivers must hold it for at least one year of unsupervised driving and follow GDL restrictions. These include limits on late-night driving and passengers, no use of hand-held or hands-free devices, and displaying GDL decals. See the NJ MVC for the current, complete list.

Step 4: Basic license

After the probationary year, and once the driver turns 18, they can upgrade to a basic license.

How parents can help

  • Keep a log of practice hours from day one. The MVC's Share the Keys guide includes one.
  • Practice in varied conditions: night, rain, highways like Route 4 and Route 17, and busy downtowns.
  • Model the behavior you expect: phone away, seat belt on, calm driving.
  • Use professional lessons for the hard parts. See our teen driver program and parent resources.
Sources: NJ MVC First Driver License page, NJ Driver Manual, and the NJ MVC announcement on 50 hours of practice driving. Rules can change; confirm at nj.gov/mvc.
